03 / What we found + what we would fix

MAKE IT EASIER
TO CHOOSE YOU.One issue. One clear fix.

Each finding below explains what we saw, why it could get in a customer’s way, and the practical change we would make.

Finding 01 / 06

The testimonial disclaimer creates doubt.

What we found

The public disclaimer says testimonials may come from people who are not actual patients. That wording does not prove any particular review is fabricated, but it leaves the reader unsure what the proof represents.

Why this can lose inquiries

A patient checking your credibility should not have to wonder which stories belong to the practice. Unclear attribution can weaken otherwise useful reviews.

Our fix

Confirm where each testimonial came from and that it can be used. Replace generic disclaimer wording with accurate, specific attribution approved by the practice, and link to original review sources where appropriate.

Proof: Public disclaimer ↗
Finding 02 / 06

An implant ad needs the full treatment explained.

What we found

Active Meta ads lead to the implant page and emphasize a consultation, a CBCT scan (a 3D dental scan) and implant placement. The page adds conditions; the full restoration and eligibility still need a clear explanation.

Why this can lose inquiries

A patient can inquire with a different idea of what the advertised treatment includes. That can create disappointment or an unproductive first conversation.

Our fix

Put a plain included / not-included summary beside the appointment button. Explain the full treatment steps and who the offer applies to, with the clinical team approving both the ad and the page.

Proof: Implant offer page ↗
Finding 03 / 06

Strong comparisons need evidence nearby.

What we found

The implant page uses “Highest Implant Success Rates” and says very few dentists reach its level of expertise. No supporting study is presented beside those statements.

Why this can lose inquiries

A patient comparing dentists needs something concrete to evaluate. A big claim without nearby support may raise more questions than it answers.

Our fix

Lead with specific training, the treatment process and evidence the practice can support. Replace broad comparisons where appropriate and have the final wording reviewed by a qualified adviser.

Proof: Treatment-page claims ↗
Finding 04 / 06

Decide which of the two websites should appear in search.

What we found

The landing homepage contains noindex / nofollow instructions, which ask search engines not to index the page or follow its links, and has no suggested search-result description. Google’s business profile links to the separate main practice site. Keeping an ad page out of search may be intentional.

Why this can lose inquiries

If a page is meant to attract local search traffic, these settings can work against that goal. If it is only for ads, changing them without a plan can create a different problem.

Our fix

Confirm the job of each website first. Keep intentional ad-page settings, and improve the headings, search descriptions, local service pages and useful blog content on the site intended to be found organically.

Proof: Fetched page metadata ↗
Finding 05 / 06

Two ad-tracking tools need a clear purpose.

What we found

The landing-site source includes two different Meta Pixel IDs. A Meta Pixel records website activity for ad measurement. We did not verify what either one records or who manages it.

Why this can lose inquiries

If the setup is unclear, the practice may struggle to tell which ads bring useful inquiries. Two IDs alone are not evidence of duplicate results.

Our fix

Confirm the owner and purpose of each pixel. Test the important steps with appropriate consent, report appointment requests separately from clicks, and remove anything only if the account review shows it is unnecessary.

Proof: Public source inspection ↗
Finding 06 / 06

The landing page’s mobile test is slow.

What we found

Google’s October 20 mobile lab test scored 51/100. The largest visible element appeared after 18.5 seconds in the simulated test. It flagged files that delay the first screen and unused scripts; no real-user dataset was available.

Why this can lose inquiries

A patient who clicks an ad may not wait for the main information to appear. That is a risk to test, not proof that every visitor experiences the same delay.

Our fix

Load the key page styles and first image sooner, postpone nonessential scripts and review unnecessary website add-ons. Repeat the same test and make sure the appointment route still works.

Proof: Mobile PageSpeed report ↗
